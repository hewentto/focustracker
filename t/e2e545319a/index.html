Building the Best Jared

Six one-tap dailies, six weekly targets. The three with a dot are the Core Three — hit those on a bad day and the day counts. Full reasoning and the training programme: PLAN.md.

Woke within ±30 min of target Anchor
The gate on everything else — including whether the 6/7am session happens. Windred 2024 (N=60,977): sleep regularity predicted mortality better than duration, and duration added nothing once regularity was in the model. Your 7.5h average is fine; the variance isn't.
Outside within 90 min of waking, 10+ min Core
The strongest causal mechanism in the whole review. Wright et al. 2013: one week of natural light advanced melatonin onset ~2 hours, within-subject, objective endpoint — and late chronotypes shifted most. Phase-resetting is daily by nature; a long Saturday walk does nothing for Tuesday.
Trained — lift, run, or stair snacks Core
One binary for all movement. Exercise logging is the only self-monitoring category that survives long-term: 61% sustained at 11 months vs 21% for diet logging. Missed the run? 3 × 30 s all-out stair climbs counts — that protocol produced +7% VO2peak from ~27 total minutes of work over 6 weeks.
Caffeine within plan
Gardiner 2023 (24 studies): −45 min total sleep, −11.4 min deep sleep. Feeds straight back into the anchor. Every-other-day dosing also parks your off-days inside the 12–51h withdrawal window, whose symptom list is your chief complaint.
Both binding blocks ran — phone and laptop
WEIS 2017 (N=455): imposed blocking +22–24% productivity; the identical tool under user control produced nothing, with 30% never switching it on. If you can dismiss it in one tap it's a dashboard.
Logged today, in writing
Harkin 2016 (138 RCTs, N=19,951): progress monitoring → goal attainment d+ = 0.40, and recorded beats unrecorded. Monitoring behaviour improves behaviour (0.79); monitoring outcomes improves outcomes (0.62). They don't cross over — which is why this counts behaviours, not tickets.

Why "missed twice" and not "don't break the chain"

Lally et al. 2010 measured what a missed day actually costs: 0.29 automaticity points, no significant long-term effect, and non-consecutive performance still built the habit. Streak-loss mechanics manufacture a failure the data says didn't happen. "Never miss twice" is supported. "Never miss once" is not.

Why the wake time is rigid and everything else isn't

Beshears et al. 2021 (Management Science) ran the only head-to-head test: rigid same-time-every-day incentives produced fewer sessions than flexible ones, and a larger drop-off afterwards. The wake time is the exception because it's a circadian anchor, not a habit slot — and because your training adherence is visibly downstream of it.

Nothing is written to the public repo — the passphrase gate protects this page, not the repo, and git history is permanent.

On the work laptop, consider skipping the token. Without it the tracker still saves locally and Download CSV still works. Create it with only Gists: Read and write and a short expiry — that scope touches your gists and nothing else.

In the gym megastudy (Nature 2021, N = 61,293, 54 expert-designed arms) expert forecasts of which interventions would work correlated with results at r = 0.02 and were 9.1× too optimistic; 45% of arms worked at 4 weeks and 8% still did at week 10. This system is ranked by evidence quality, which is knowable — not by what will work for you, which isn't.
