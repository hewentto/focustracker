Five behaviours, ranked by evidence quality. Track the behaviour, not the outcome — that's the moderator that matters (Harkin et al. 2016, 138 RCTs, N = 19,951).

Log — saved locally

Woke within ±30 min of target Tier 1
Your ~7.5h average means duration isn't the problem — variable start time is. Windred et al. 2024 (UK Biobank, N=60,977) found the Sleep Regularity Index predicted mortality better than duration, and with regularity in the model duration added nothing significant. Phillips et al. 2017 found irregular sleepers had identical total sleep time but melatonin onset ~2.6h later. Anchor the wake time — it sets circadian phase; bedtime follows it.
Caffeine within plan (dose, time, sugar-free) Tier 1
Gardiner et al. 2023 (24 studies): pooled cost is −45 min total sleep, −7% efficiency, −11.4 min deep sleep. Their dose-scaled cutoff puts ~160 mg at roughly 12:05pm for an 11pm bedtime. Two bigger levers than the 25 minutes: guarana adds unlabelled caffeine, and every-other-day dosing parks your off-days inside the 12–51h withdrawal window, whose validated symptoms are "decreased energy, decreased alertness, difficulty concentrating, foggy" (Juliano & Griffiths 2004).
Ran both binding blocks (phone and laptop) Tier 1
The sharpest asymmetry in the literature: imposed blocking produced +22–24% productivity; the identical tool under user control produced nothing, with 30% never switching it on (WEIS 2017, N=455). Binding daily limits sustained −22 min/day over 12 weeks with minimal decay (Allcott, Gentzkow & Song 2022, N=1,933). If you can dismiss it in one tap it's a dashboard. Block the laptop too — Heitmayer 2025 found phone-distance halved phone use while computer leisure rose 27→66 min, with no net change in work time.
15–20 min walk at the afternoon dip Tier 2
Acute exercise → feelings of energy Δ = 0.47 (Loy, O'Connor & Dishman 2013, 16 studies) — moderate, homogeneous, and the most robust result in the exercise literature. Its effect on fatigue was Δ = 0.03 (null) except at light-to-moderate intensity for ≥20 min in already-fatigued people. In the closest-matched RCT the benefit was independent of fitness gains — an easy walk qualifies. Don't expect cognitive enhancement; that claim doesn't survive bias correction.
Logged today, in writing Tier 2
Harkin et al. 2016 (138 RCTs, N=19,951): progress monitoring → goal attainment d+ = 0.40. Moderators are precise: physically recorded (0.43) beats not (0.29); reported/public (0.47–0.55) beats private (0.19); and monitoring behaviour improves behaviour (0.79) while monitoring outcomes improves outcomes (0.62) — they don't cross over. Hence: behaviours, not tickets closed.

Why "missed twice" and not "don't break the chain"

Lally et al. 2010 — the study everyone cites for habit formation — measured what a missed day actually costs: 0.29 automaticity points, no significant long-term effect, and non-consecutive performance still produced gains. Streak purism is contradicted by the very paper used to justify it. The "what-the-hell effect" is real in abstinence-rule contexts (dieting, sobriety) where the rule itself creates the violation; extending it to skipping a walk is unwarranted. "Never miss twice" is supported. "Never miss once" is not.

Why the daily slot is deliberately flexible (and the wake time isn't)

Beshears, Lee, Milkman, Mislavsky & Wisdom (2021, Management Science) ran the only head-to-head test: rigid same-time-every-day incentives produced fewer sessions than flexible ones, and among people exercising at similar rates the rigid group showed a larger drop-off afterwards. Rigid timing raises the failure rate on days life interferes, and each failure costs more than the marginal automaticity gain. The wake time is the exception — that's a circadian anchor, not a habit slot, and it does need to be fixed.

Storage & syncnot configured

Your log saves automatically in this browser. To keep it in a real file and share it between devices, paste a GitHub token below once per device — each sync writes two files into a secret Gist on your account: focus-tracker-data.json (used for merging) and focus-log.csv (opens in Excel or Sheets). Nothing is ever written to the public repo — the passphrase gate protects this page, not the repo, and git history is permanent.

On your work laptop, consider skipping the token. It lives in that browser's local storage, which is fine on a personal device and a judgement call on a managed one. Without a token the tracker still saves locally and Download CSV still works — you just won't get cross-device sync there. Create the token with only the account permission Gists: Read and write and a short expiry; that scope can read and write your gists and nothing else — no repos, no code, no account settings.
How to create the token (60 seconds)

GitHub → Settings → Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens → Generate new token. Set an expiry, skip repository access entirely, then under Account permissions set Gists → Read and write. Generate, copy, paste it above, press Save token & sync now. The first sync creates the secret gist and fills in the Gist ID for you — copy that ID to your other device so both point at the same gist.

What this is not: a plan that will definitely work. In the Milkman et al. gym megastudy (Nature 2021, N = 61,293, 54 arms) expert forecasts of which interventions would succeed correlated with actual results at r = 0.02 and were 9.1× too optimistic; 45% of arms worked at 4 weeks and 8% still did at week 10. These five are ranked by evidence quality, which is knowable — not by what will work for you, which isn't. Expect decay, expect to swap items out, and treat a missed day as data rather than a verdict.
